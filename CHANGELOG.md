## [Unreleased]

## [4.3.2] - 2026-11-07

### Added

- Two exam question types in Learn (2026-11-07). "Opposite in meaning" shows a word and asks for its antonym among four, as the GRE and TEM-4 do; one wrong option is a synonym of the word when its entry lists one, since mixing up the direction is the usual slip. Word formation shows a sentence with a gap and a related word in capitals ("DECREPITUDE" for "decrepit"), and you type the form that fits, as in Cambridge B2 First and C1 Advanced. Both come to words you hold for days or longer, after easier questions have gone right.
- Hover inside Phevere (2026-11-07): resting the pointer on a word in any Phevere window now counts as hovering it, as in any other app. That covers the main window, Settings, the clipboard window, the lookup pop-up's own entry, the quick translation panel, the scanned-text window and web pages Phevere opens. Button, tab and menu labels count too; only text fields you type in are skipped. With Selection set to Shortcut, the trigger looks up the word you rest on; before, it fell back to the last thing you had selected. With Selection on, resting on a word in an open pop-up shows that word in it, and the pop-up stays where it is.

### Changed

- A new Progress page (2026-11-07): Learn → Progress was rebuilt from scratch as plain panels that answer one question each. Today shows answers given and the share right, Reviews and New words as bars (a goal you passed reads "42 · goal 10" instead of "42 of 10"), and the week's study days as dots beside the streak. Words leads with the words learned (Young or Mature) of those started, the four stages as one bar whose key opens each stage's words, and how many were started and learned each day since you began. Memory shows the share remembered now, the share of reviews right over 30 days against the 90% aim, the average recall over the next month without review, and the words fading first. Reviews ahead covers two weeks, Study days is a 26-week calendar, and Skills, Trouble words and Lists follow. In a wide window the smaller panels pair up. Root families and the answers-per-day and hold-length charts left the page.
- More typed questions (2026-11-07): Learn now asks you to type a word once you would get it right about three times in four (it aimed at 85% before), and spelling's share of a session rises from 35% to 40%. For a learner whose typed answers go wrong more often than expected, the aim moves back towards 85% and the share shrinks, so typing never turns into a run of misses.
- Pronunciations as learner's dictionaries print them (2026-11-07): British first, then American, one each, and at most one more variant. Each accent leads with the transcription most dictionaries agree on, an unlabelled English transcription is filed under the accent its sounds show (ɒ and əʊ are British; ɚ, oʊ and an r before a consonant are American), and one that only repeats a shown one with syllable dots or length marks is dropped. "moratorium" shows UK and US instead of five chips. Saved words show their readings in the same order.
- A word in one language other than English names it (2026-11-07): the lookup shows the language as a label under the headword ("German" for "tschüss"), as the language row does for words in several languages.
- Saved words follow their lookup (2026-11-07): a saved word's definition, sources and pronunciations are replaced when a later lookup of the word has heard from every dictionary, and opening a row in Words looks the word up again in the background. A row no longer keeps senses from an early, partial lookup that the lookup itself does not show, English senses come before a gloss in another language, and its Dictionary line names sources as the lookup does ("Webster 1913").

### Fixed

- The trigger looks up the word under the pointer inside Phevere (2026-11-07): with Selection set to Shortcut, pressing the trigger over a word in a Phevere window did nothing once the pointer had moved a little along the word or a minute had passed, because the check that reads the word under the pointer in other apps skips Phevere's own windows. The trigger now asks the Phevere page under the pointer for the word there, focused or not.
- Saved words keep senses from another computer's dictionaries (2026-11-07): a lookup on a computer without an offline dictionary, such as Webster 1913, replaced a saved word's definition and dropped the senses that dictionary had given on the computer that saved it ("decrepit" lost "Broken down with age"). A row with senses from an offline dictionary or a keyed service that the lookup did not have is now left as it is.
- Symbols are read by name (2026-11-07): a saved mathematical letter such as 𝜓 was spelled out as "letter one D seven one three"; the voice now reads it as the plain letter, psi.
- Saved sentences play (2026-11-07): the speaker on a saved sentence said "Could not play pronunciation" when the sentence had a stray kana in Chinese text, or was Japanese, which no bundled voice reads. Chinese with a stray kana is read as Chinese, the row's language is passed to the voice, and a language without a bundled voice uses an installed system voice or Google Translate's voice, as the Translation tab does. A sentence no longer waits for a dictionary lookup before it speaks.
- Words in other languages sound human (2026-11-07): the pronunciation chip of a German, French or other non-English word read the transcription with English sounds, and the speaker fell back to the mechanical eSpeak voice. Both now play the word's recording, else a system voice or Google Translate's voice for its language, with eSpeak last.
- "Closest in meaning" answers count for the Meaning skill on Progress; they were stored without a skill.

## [4.3.1] - 2026-11-07

### Added

- Zoom in on a sense picture (2026-11-07): the large picture that opens from a sense can be zoomed and moved. On the computer, scroll over it to zoom around the pointer, double-click to switch between fit and 2.5×, drag to move a zoomed picture, and press +, − or 0; on the phone, pinch, double-tap and drag. It zooms up to 6× and never smaller than fit or off its frame. Clicking or tapping the picture no longer closes it; a click beside it, Escape or Back does.
- "Closest in meaning" questions (2026-11-07): Learn can now show a word and ask which of four words is closest to it in meaning, the vocabulary item of TOEFL reading and the GRE. The right answer is a synonym from the word's dictionary entry, under its main part of speech; the wrong ones are other words from your lists that look like that synonym, so the odd one out is never the answer, and a word that is itself a synonym is never offered as wrong. It comes up for words you are learning or hold for days, once you have answered a few meaning questions right, and like the other kinds it is picked by how likely you are to get it right. The countdown applies to it as to the other choice questions. Desktop and phone alike.
- Learn more once today is done (2026-11-07): when Learn says "Nothing due today", it now offers "Learn 10 more words", which starts a round of the next new words past today's goal (the number is your daily goal, at least five), and "Review 20 words early", which asks the started words that are due soonest. Each shows only when it has words. They work on My words, a word book and All books, on the desktop and the phone.
- Search shortcuts (2026-11-07): Ctrl+F and Ctrl+K (Cmd+F and Cmd+K on a Mac) put the cursor in the main window's search box from Words, Learn or Recent.

### Changed

- Chinese glosses have their own section (2026-11-07): an English word's Chinese equivalents from Chinese Wiktionary (and glosses in Japanese or Russian from those editions) are no longer numbered in among its English senses. They follow the English senses in their own section, "In Chinese" (中文释义 in Chinese), with its own "Definitions from Chinese Wiktionary" line, and the section rail has a Chinese tab to jump to it; the English senses' line names only the English sources. A word with only Chinese glosses shows them as its senses, as before. Desktop and phone alike.
- The language row keeps its order (2026-11-07): picking a language under the headword no longer moves its chip to second place; English stays first and the rest keep the page's order. Only a language folded under "+N" is brought into view when it is the one shown.
- Learn settings you tap instead of spin (2026-11-07): Settings → Learn no longer uses number wheels. Each list, My words and every word book you added, has a row of choices (5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 50 new words a day) and an Other field for any number from 1 to 999. Under the choices a line says what the goal costs, "About 13 min a day", and when the last word in the list will have been started, "every word started by Dec 30", and it changes as soon as you pick another number; the row's corner shows how many words are not started yet. Once Phevere knows your pace from three or more study days, the number that fits your usual day is among the choices, marked Your pace. The countdown on choice questions is a row of choices too: Off, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20 and 30 seconds. Desktop and phone alike.
- A denser Progress page (2026-11-07): Learn → Progress no longer sets its figures as posters. The streak, Memory, Reviews ahead, Answers and Skills numbers are about a third of their old size, sections sit closer together, Answers per day and Skills share a row on a wide window as Reviews ahead and How long words are held do, and charts stop growing past a readable width. A desktop window now shows about two sections at once instead of one number.
- Updates are on About (2026-11-07): Settings no longer has a separate Updates page. The update status, Install updates, Check now and, on the computer, Roll back now sit on Settings → About, right under the version, and opening About checks for an update. On the phone this shows on Android only, as before.
- No "i" on card pictures (2026-11-07): pictures in Words → Cards and Learn no longer carry the small "i" button and its credit strip. On the computer the author and licence show when you hover the picture; on the phone they show in the full-screen picture. Settings → About still credits Wikimedia Commons.

### Fixed

- The language switch moves and stays usable (2026-11-07): picking Romanian, French or another language under the headword now marks that language as the one shown, so the highlight moves and English stays one tap away. The entry shown is that language's own Wiktionary section, even when it only glosses the word with its English twin, as Romanian "decrepit" does; before, Phevere swapped in the English senses and left English highlighted, so the row looked stuck.
- Rich senses no longer drop out for minutes (2026-11-07): looking up something Wiktionary has no page for, such as a stray string on the clipboard, counted as Wiktionary failing, and after two such misses Phevere skipped Wiktionary for three minutes or more. Every lookup in that time had only Free Dictionary's plain senses, without the grammar codes, usage labels, synonyms and examples Wiktionary gives, which is why some words looked detailed and others bare. Only a real network failure counts now.
- Words list and export no longer freeze on Windows (2026-11-07): with another program reading windows through Windows accessibility, such as an automation or AI agent tool, Phevere's own accessibility tree was queried hundreds of thousands of times a second, and scrolling, hovering and clicking in Words stalled for seconds at a time. Phevere now builds that tree only while a screen reader such as Narrator, NVDA or JAWS is running; to keep it on for another assistive tool, create an empty file named `.enable-accessibility` in `%APPDATA%\phevere` and restart. Hover lookup also leaves Phevere's own windows and dialogs alone: resting the pointer on the Export or Export for Anki save dialog used to block the whole app for up to a minute.

## [4.3.0] - 2026-11-06

### Added

- Phevere in your language (2026-11-06): the whole interface, on the computer and the phone, now comes in English, 简体中文, 繁體中文, 日本語, 한국어, Español, Français, Deutsch, Русский and Português (Brasil). Settings opens with an App language menu: System, the default, follows your computer's or phone's language and falls back to English when Phevere does not have it; each language is listed by its own name so you can find yours whatever the screen shows. A new choice applies at once. On the phone everything repaints; on the computer the tray menu and the app menu change, and the main window, Settings, the lookup pop-up and the scan windows reload in the new language, except a region scan you are in the middle of, which changes in place. Counts, numbers, percentages, sizes and dates follow the language's own rules ("1 слово", "5 слов", "42 %"), and language names, such as a translation's target or an entry's language, are written in your language too. Definitions, examples, word history and anything else that comes from a dictionary stay as the dictionary wrote them, and so do your own words and notes. The Windows installer is still in English.
- Translate a scanned picture in place (2026-11-06): the scanned-text window on the computer and the Scan screen on the phone have a Translate button. It translates the text of the picture paragraph by paragraph, so sentences read naturally, and paints each translation over the original, on the background sampled around it (a flat colour on a screenshot, the surrounding texture on a photo), in the original's colour, weight and serif or sans style, shrunk to fit the paragraph's space rather than spilling over. A short label whose translation is much longer, such as a two-character Chinese heading in English, runs on into the empty space beside it at a readable size instead. Paragraphs appear as their translations arrive, the ones on screen first, and a long page takes a handful of requests rather than one a paragraph, so translating a picture does not hold up the Translation tab in Lookup. Turning Translate off or closing the picture stops what has not been asked yet. Text already in your language, prices, numbers, the figures in a table and program code are left as they are, so a page that mixes two languages only changes where it needs to. Vertical Chinese or Japanese stays vertical when translated into Chinese or Japanese, and a vertical column translated into English runs down the column. Press Translate again to see the original at once, and again for the translation. The translated text can be selected, copied and looked up, Copy all copies the translation in reading order, the Text view shows it, and Save image saves the translated picture at full size. A paragraph that could not be translated keeps its original with a dashed outline, and Retry asks again. The language is your quick translation target from Settings → Shortcuts on the computer and the Translation language on the phone (Auto picks English for a page in another language and Chinese for an English page); the language menu next to Translate changes it.
- Open lookups as a full pop-up (2026-11-06): Settings → Capture has "Open lookups as", with Strip (as before, and the default) and Full pop-up. With Full pop-up, a word you select, hover or look up with the trigger shortcut opens straight in the full lookup, with no strip to click first. The pop-up sits below the word, or above it when the word is near the bottom of the screen, and never covers it; when neither side has room for the whole pop-up it takes the larger side and is a little shorter. It keeps its side of the word while the entry loads. It stays on the monitor the word is on, also with monitors at different scaling. The next word you look up shows in the same pop-up, which moves to that word, unless you have dragged the pop-up somewhere, in which case it stays there. A word you select inside the pop-up is looked up in it, and Back returns to the entry you were reading. Hover lookup leaves the pop-up's own text alone, so the entry stays while you read it; the trigger shortcut over a word there looks that word up. The pop-up's own options no longer have the "Open full lookup panel" switch, since this choice now lives only in Settings; if you had turned that switch on, Phevere keeps it as Full pop-up. Desktop only, because the phone already opens the full pop-up.
- Copy on word rows (2026-11-06): every row in My words, a word book, All books and Recent now has a Copy button beside Play and Look up. One click or tap copies the word exactly as saved; the button turns into a tick for a moment and a screen reader hears "Copied", and the row does not open or look the word up. An opened row in My words also has "Copy with meaning", which copies the word with its pronunciation, part of speech and numbered senses, ready to paste into notes. On a phone narrower than 400 points the clock time on each row and the "New" chip on book rows are hidden, so long words keep their line; the day headings, the saved date in an opened row, and the Learning and Known chips still show. Desktop and phone alike.
- Scanned text in place (2026-11-06): a scanned image now opens as the picture itself with every piece of recognised text turned into real text at the same place, size and angle, the way Live Text and Google Lens show it. You can select words on the picture, copy them, and look them up as you would anywhere else in Phevere. Text switches to the recognised text alone, paragraph by paragraph. Edit lets you change any line, on the picture or in Text; a changed line is painted over the picture in the line's own colours, at the letter widths the old text had, and in bold or a serif face where the old text was. Copy all copies the text in reading order, and Save image saves the picture with your changes (Copy image as well on the computer). Words the recognition was unsure about keep a dotted underline instead of being left out. On the computer this opens in its own window for pasted, dropped and clipboard images, a scanned window, and a scanned region with more than one line; a single-line region is still looked up straight away. On the phone it is the Scan screen.
- Grammar codes and usage notes in senses (2026-11-06): each sense now starts with its grammar in square brackets, such as [countable], [transitive] or [intransitive; with on or upon], in small teal type, followed by its usage labels in italic brackets, such as (informal, chiefly UK) or (law), the way the Oxford Learner's and Longman dictionaries set them. A noun whose senses are all one kind says so once after its heading, as in "noun [usually uncountable]". Under a sense you may now find its synonyms and opposites as words you can tap, and phrases it is often used in, with the word itself in bold. These come from Wiktionary's own labels, which the definitions Phevere showed before had left out. Desktop and phone alike.
- Phrases section (2026-11-06): phrasal verbs, idioms and other set phrases with the word have their own section in the Lexicon, after Synonyms and before Word family. Phrasal verbs such as "give up" and "give in" come first. Within each group the phrases people use most lead, as "give way" and "give birth" do under "give", and phrases with no recorded use, such as "give dap" or "give hat", wait under "All" however short they are.
- Language switch (2026-11-06): a word that Wiktionary has in several languages shows them as a row under the pronunciation, with English first. Tap French, Italian or any other to see the word's entry in that language, without the English senses or English pronunciation; tap English to go back. On the desktop a long list folds after seven names under "+N"; on the phone the row scrolls sideways.
- New Learn questions (2026-11-06): "Choose the word for the gap" shows a real example sentence for the word, from your note, the sentence you saved it from or the dictionary, with the word blanked out, and you pick it from four words of the same part of speech that all take the same form, so "Lanterns hung from every branch" offers only plurals. A word whose definition names the answer, or that the answer's definition names, is never one of the wrong options. "Spell the word" shows the meaning and the word's own letters shuffled in a row; tap them in order, tap a placed letter to take it back, or type the letters and use Backspace on a keyboard. Both come up for words you are learning or hold for days, when the word has a sentence or is a single word of 3 to 14 letters, and the quiz picks them like the other kinds, by how likely you are to get them right. Desktop and phone alike.
- About page (2026-11-06): Settings has an About page on the desktop and on the phone. It shows the version you have installed, with a Copy button for bug reports, and links to the source code on GitHub, a button to star it, the changelog, a page for reporting a problem that already names your version and system, the privacy policy, the MIT licence and the author. It also credits every dictionary, word list, voice, model and font Phevere uses, each with its licence, so Wiktionary and the other CC BY-SA sources are properly attributed. On a Mac, Phevere → About Phevere opens this page.
- README in other languages (2026-11-06): the README is now also in Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French, German, Russian and Brazilian Portuguese, with a language switcher at the top of each.
- Apps in Sources (2026-11-06): Words → Sources now also groups words saved from an app that shows no web address or document, such as a chat app, under the app's name. On Android the row shows the app's own icon; elsewhere it shows the app's first letter. Like a site or a PDF, an app's words can be made into a word book.

### Changed

- Learn skips what is not a word (2026-11-06): sentences, symbols and other entries that are not words or phrases stay in My words, Cards and Sources but no longer appear in Learn. They do not take today's new-word slots, are not asked in a round, and do not count in Today's new and due numbers, the stages, Progress or the reviews forecast. This covers a line you looked up as a sentence, a sign such as "𝜓", a code the dictionary files as a symbol such as "LIL", and an entry whose only meaning is "Translation from JA to ZH" or the word repeated back. Phrases and idioms such as "cease and desist" and "give up" are still studied, and so is a word in another language that has a real meaning. Desktop and phone alike.
- Pictures on every card (2026-11-06): a card in Words → Cards and in Learn now shows a picture whenever the word has one anywhere, not only when a picture fits the meaning you saved. It takes the picture on the word's Wiktionary entry first, then the picture of the Wikipedia article on the word, then a picture tied to another meaning, and searches Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons for the word when none of these has one. Only a word with no picture anywhere keeps its letter. On a 577-word notebook, 537 cards now have a picture instead of 131. A lookup still shows a picture only beside the meaning it fits.
- Reading order of scanned text (2026-11-06): recognised text now reads as a person reads the image. Words join into lines and lines into paragraphs, a two-column page is read column by column instead of across both columns, a page photographed at an angle reads the same as a flat one, vertical Chinese reads from the right-hand column, a word broken with a hyphen at a line end is joined again, and a copied paragraph comes out as its sentences on one line. Text the recognition was unsure about is kept instead of dropped. On six test images (a web page, a two-column essay, a photographed page, a slide, mixed Chinese and English, and vertical Chinese), the words in correct order rose from 81% to over 99%, and whole paragraphs found from 11 of 33 to 33 of 33. Text that runs beside a picture and continues below it stays one paragraph: on real browser screenshots (English Wikipedia in light and dark mode, Chinese Wikipedia, a Wikipedia list page and a Chinese government page), long paragraphs found whole rose from 17 of 39 to 24 of 39, and all 7 on the Chinese Wikipedia page. Icons are no longer read as stray letters, and a large photo is read in about half the time.
- Cards (2026-11-06): once a pile is poured out, clicking its first card again gathers the cards back onto the pile, as the pile's label already did. That card's picture still turns it over, and the Look up button on its back opens the lookup. Every other poured-out card works as before: its text opens the lookup.
- Settings → Learn (2026-11-06): new words a day and the countdown on choice questions are set on number wheels instead of the 5, 10, 20 and 40 buttons, the Other field and the countdown menu. Each list and the countdown has a card with one large number and its neighbours above and below. Drag or flick it up and down, scroll it with the mouse wheel or a trackpad (a page scroll that passes over a card keeps scrolling the page), tap above or below to step one, or tap the number to type any value. From the keyboard, the arrow keys step by one, Page Up and Page Down by 10 (5 on the countdown), and Home and End go to the ends. New words a day still runs from 1 to 999, and the countdown from Off through 5 to 30 seconds, now in steps of one second. Desktop and phone alike.
- Learn → Progress (2026-11-06): the large numbers no longer run into the rules, the labels or each other, at any window width and on the phone; a long number such as a four-digit streak shrinks to fit its column. The sentences are gone. Each number has a short label under it, such as "Day streak" with "Best 3", "Remembered" with "of 37 words" and "20 due", "Due in 7 days" and "Mature" with "of 36 started". Today's new words and reviews sit beside the streak as two bars, with a third for the share answered right once you have answered something.
- Hints in typed questions (2026-11-06): a typed question now starts with the first letter only, however weakly you hold the word, instead of up to four letters for a long word; a word you hold weakly gets an easier kind of question instead. Each Hint gives one more letter and lowers the credit for your answer: any hint makes it Hard at most, and needing hints for more than a third of the hidden letters makes it Again. "I knew it" after such an answer makes it Hard.
- Typos in typed answers (2026-11-06): a typed answer with one slip in a word now counts as right, as on Duolingo. A slip is one letter missing, one extra, one wrong, or two neighbouring letters swapped, in a word of three letters or more, and it must not spell another word: "strt" and "satrt" pass for "start", but "star", "starts" and "stret" do not. The other national spelling counts as right ("colour" for "color", "centre" for "center"), and a space or hyphen missed inside a phrase ("icecream" for "ice cream") is a typo. The answer gets the tick and Good, never Easy, and "You have a typo" shows the right spelling with the letters you got wrong underlined. Hints lower the credit as before.
- README download section (2026-11-06): the table of download buttons is gone. The section links to the latest release and lists the system each platform needs.
- Translating a sentence (2026-11-06): the Translation tab shows the first engine's translation as soon as it arrives, and a second engine's translation joins it when that one answers, instead of the tab waiting for every engine.

### Fixed

- The app freezing during a lookup when Wiktionary is blocked (2026-11-06): when the check for whether a word is a form of another failed at once, because Wiktionary or Kaikki could not be reached, the lookup kept re-checking in a tight loop until its wait ran out. The pop-up, the main window or the phone app could stop responding for up to 17 seconds, and a pressed speaker stayed silent until the loop ended. The lookup now stops waiting as soon as the check fails. Desktop and phone alike.
- Lower-case translations (2026-11-06): a word or short phrase now translates in lower case when you selected it in lower case or in a language without capitals, as dictionaries do: 苹果 gives "apple", 猫 "cat" and French "chat" "cat", where the translation used to read "Apple" and "Cat" as if it began a sentence. Names, languages, days and months, acronyms, words such as "iPhone" and the pronoun "I" keep their capitals (北京 still gives "Beijing", 英语 "English", 星期一 "Monday"), and so do sentences and German nouns. This applies to quick translation, the Translation tab of a lookup and translated scans, on the desktop and the phone.
- A word opening as one rare sense on a slow connection (2026-11-06): when the dictionaries took longer than two and a half seconds to answer but WordNet's glosses had already come, a lookup could open with those glosses alone, such as "cat" as "A spiteful woman gossip.", and the dictionaries' senses and pronunciation only joined a moment later. The lookup now waits for the dictionaries as it does when nothing has come yet, so the word opens with its full entry. Desktop and phone alike.
- Sources after the lookup shortcut (2026-11-06): a word selected first and then looked up with the trigger shortcut now records the page or app it came from, so it shows in Words → Sources. Before, only a word selected while the shortcut was held kept its source.
- Pictures that vanished (2026-11-06): a sense picture no longer appears as an empty grey or dark box that then disappears. Its place stays blank until the picture has arrived, and then it fades in. A picture whose download fails is tried again after one second and again after three; on the phone a last try goes through the app's own connection, which gets through more often on a weak network. Only a picture that fails all of these is left out. A slow picture keeps loading for as long as it takes. This applies to the desktop pop-up, phone lookups and the cards in Words.
- Card pictures on the desktop (2026-11-06): a card in Words → Cards shows its picture as soon as the first source finds it, as the phone already did, instead of waiting for every source. On 80 notebook words over a simulated normal connection, a card's picture is chosen after about 1.3 seconds instead of 2.1, and on a slow one (one to three seconds a request) after 5.6 seconds instead of 9.5.
- Pictures held up at start (2026-11-06): when the first few picture requests after opening Phevere hang, the pictures behind them no longer wait until those requests give up. In a test with three requests hanging at the start, the next picture's details arrived after 3.6 seconds instead of 8.3.
- Cards scrolled past (2026-11-06): when you scroll through Cards, the cards you scrolled past stop looking for their pictures, so the cards you stop at go first. In a test where four cards were passed and four came to rest, the resting cards' pictures were chosen after 1.8 seconds instead of 4.3 on a normal connection, and after 7.6 seconds instead of 18.5 on a slow one. A card that was passed, or whose search failed, looks again when it is back on screen.
- A box such as "English Wikipedia has an article on: proposition" no longer appears inside a sense (2026-11-06). Wiktionary's links to Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, Wikispecies and its other sister projects are left out of definitions, including in words you looked up before this change.
- "i.e." and other words whose letters look foreign now open in English when Wiktionary has an English entry for them (2026-11-06). "i.e." used to show only its Italian sense.
- The heart on an entry opened in another language saves and removes that language's word (2026-11-06). On the Danish entry of "give", the heart used to show the English "give" as saved and remove it when tapped. Now the English and the Danish "give" are two words in My words, each marked with its language. Look up on a saved word, its card and its review question open it in its own language. Importing My words from another device adds a word saved in a second language instead of skipping it, sync keeps the two apart, and Learn asks both. "Add to word book" is not offered on an entry opened in another language when the word also has an English entry, because a word book keeps spellings only.
